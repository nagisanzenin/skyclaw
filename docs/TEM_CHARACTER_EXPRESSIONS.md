# Tem — hair, face and expression reference

Creator correction, September 9, 2026: the new spiky black ring around a large white forehead looked bald. Prefer the pretty, feminine-coded but tousled hairstyle in the original Gaze artwork, with an eccentric-genius energy. Preserve the original expressive faces across the new cozy Den series.

## References inspected

- [Original Gaze](../assets/tem-gaze-overview.png): the central operator is the primary hair/face reference chosen by the creator. Dark hair has a substantial crown, a diagonal parted fringe and long side locks; the face sits within it rather than inside an even black halo. The small calm smile and compact colored eyes stay readable among busy screens.
- [Original Anima](../assets/tem-anima-overview.png): right-side technical-lead Tem and curious-student Tem show intentional attitude differences. The former has angled, mischievous/determined eyes and a compact smirk; the latter uses a gentler open gaze. Their upright/quadruped poses contribute to the contrast.
- [Original Conscious](../assets/tem-conscious-overview.png): the observer heads and small character-spec figure retain the same framed face, understated mouth and scarf. The artwork labels a WORK mode; the label alone is not evidence of a large visible facial transformation.
- [Original Many Tems](../assets/tem-swarm-overview.png) and [Tem-Code](../assets/tem-code-overview.png): activity, head angle, tools and posture make the creatures lively. Preserve that storytelling rather than repeating one front-facing face.

These are visual references only. Their historical product/performance claims are not adopted into new illustrations.

## Hair construction

1. Start with a full, softly irregular dark crown between the ears. It must visibly cover the scalp, not be a thin rim behind a white oval.
2. Bring a parted or side-swept fringe diagonally down over the upper forehead, creating an asymmetric smaller face opening. Leave enough visible fur for the gold1mark; it can sit on the cheek/body when the fringe covers that area.
3. Add layered cheek-framing locks extending toward the jaw/shoulders, with a few longer trailing clumps. Read as a pretty messy shag/long bob, not spikes radiating uniformly in every direction.
4. Ears emerge naturally through the hair. Use a few flyaway tufts and asymmetry for the scrappy eccentric-genius look. Do not turn the silhouette into a circular mane, helmet, receding hairline or perfectly symmetrical wig.
5. Maintain chunky pixel construction. The request for feminine hair is about the hairstyle; retain the simple animal face and Tem identity, without adding human anatomy, makeup or large anime eyes.

## Face construction

Keep the small white face bounded by the fringe and side locks. The eyes are compact amber-left/cyan-right marks, not huge black circles or oversized glittering eyes. The nose is tiny. The mouth is a small cat-like paired curve/smirk; avoid a giant moustache-shaped W, broad human teeth or the same open grin on every character.

Emotion can come from a few pixels: eyelid angle, one raised brow, mouth corner, ear tilt and head pitch. Body posture and the other Tem's reaction complete it. Do not erase heterochromia to make an expression; closed eyes can naturally become dark curved lines, with identity retained elsewhere.

## Expression vocabulary

The first three directions are grounded in the inspected references. Others are proposed scene variations using the same face grammar, not claims that an identical sprite exists in the old set.

| Expression | Face / posture direction | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Warm curiosity | Compact open eyes, tiny relaxed :3, head tilt, ears attentive | Exploring, helping, learning |
| Mischievous determination | Slightly angled eyelids/brows, small off-center >:3 smirk, lean forward | Coding, problem solving, a clever plan |
| Calm operator | Focused small gaze toward task, restrained smile, relaxed shoulders | Gaze/terminal controls |
| Aha | Eyes a little brighter/open, ears perked, small delighted mouth, lifted paw | Discovering a connection |
| Skeptical | One raised brow, slight sideways glance, one mouth corner lifted | Checking evidence, friendly disagreement |
| Gleeful tinkerer | Uneven excited gaze and mischievous grin, messy flyaways, energetic pose | Playful experiments; eccentric, never a medical caricature |
| Concentrating | Lowered lids or subtle brow, tiny neutral/smirk mouth, still posture | Reading, detailed repairs |
| Sleepy/content | Closed curved eyes, tiny relaxed smile, curled body | Cozy rest among companions |

In a group, assign an expression and gaze target to each Tem before generation. At least several faces should differ in mood/head angle. A litter feels alive because members react to each other, not because one sprite was cloned seven times.

## Review gate

Before replacing the full series, inspect the hair/face prototype against Gaze and Anima. Check crown coverage, diagonal fringe, side-lock length, eye size/colors and small mouth. Then check at normal README width, not only zoomed in. Every production prompt must include the original Gaze hair reference and preserve the expression vocabulary. A cozy background cannot compensate for a wrong character face.

## Approved model sheet and body construction

The creator accepted the [character study](../assets/character/tem-character-study.png) and requested a local copy. It combines the original Gaze hair/face with Anima's slender biped and quadruped silhouettes. Use it as the primary character reference for new images; the cozy Den banner is the environment reference only.

Biped: narrow torso roughly half the head width, small shoulders/waist, distinct slim arms and short legs, lightweight paws. Quadruped: lean horizontal torso with a clear back line, four appropriately attached slender legs and flexible kitten balance. These are drawing targets, not anatomical claims or pixel measurements of every original asset. Avoid circular bellies, thick thighs, inflated plush-toy bodies and giant square feet.

Ground every pose: feet/paws contact the same perspective plane as nearby props; add contact shadows, plausible scale and consistent warm/cool scene lighting. Body and environment should use compatible pixel scale and edge treatment. A white body may pick up restrained warm/lavender scene light; pure white sticker-like cutouts and thick halos are not acceptable. Separate base-sprite palette rules from in-scene illumination.

The generated sheet is a visual reference; written canonical palette and identity rules take precedence over incidental generated swatch differences or extra text. Review in-scene results against it before installing a full batch.
