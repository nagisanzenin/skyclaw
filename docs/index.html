01 / START WITH AN ORDINARY MOMENT

You leave.
What continues?

Imagine giving Tem a project to investigate before you go to sleep. You want to return in the morning to useful findings—and to a companion that still understands what you were trying to do.

That is the standard Temm1e is being built toward. Start with the simplest version: one request to a language model. It can reason about the project, propose a plan and return a verdict. Then the call ends.

Where does the plan live now? What carries out the next action? What remembers the result? If the computer restarts at three in the morning, what connects the work before the interruption to the work after it?

An LLM supplies a moment of intelligence. Temm1e is an attempt to give those moments a continuing existence.

This is the creator’s entity-oriented vision. The model supplies reasoning, intelligence and verdicts one API call at a time. The harness supplies the environment, memory, temporal machinery and durable records that connect those calls.

Follow the overnight project a little further. Each difficulty it encounters explains another part of Tem—and why the mathematics belongs there.

02 / A THOUGHT BECOMES AN ACTION

First, it needs a world.

Tem has a plan. To make progress, it must encounter something outside that plan: open the repository, inspect a page, run a command, read an error. The next thought should be informed by what actually happened.

That is why computer use matters to this project. Prowl lets Tem interact with a browser and retain a dedicated authenticated profile. Gaze gives it screenshots and input actions on the desktop. Shell, file and Git tools connect it to the project’s working materials.

The aim is to participate in a continuing digital environment, as a person does. Sometimes a browser is appropriate; sometimes a terminal or API is more direct. What matters is that the action and its consequence become part of one history.

A click alone does not establish success. Tem needs the resulting page state. A command needs its actual output. These observations give the next model call something more substantial than the previous model’s expectation.

An investigation can branch into several pieces of work. Many Tems explores how workers can coordinate through shared signals. That creates a practical question: which worker should take which ready task? Hive makes its selection policy explicit.

The mathematics of cooperation: Many Tems
S = Aα Uβ (1 − D)γ (1 − F)δ Rζ

Affinity, urgency, difficulty, failure and reward signals combine into a priority score. Exponents control their influence. The implementation floors affinity, urgency and reward at 0.001, and clamps difficulty and failure into [0,0.999]. Dependency checks and task claims remain separate responsibilities.

This is a coordination heuristic, not a proof of optimal scheduling, fairness or a speedup over one worker. More workers do not eliminate the need to inspect the resulting work.

But an overnight project will produce more observations than one context window can hold. Once Tem can act, it immediately faces a second problem: deciding what to carry forward.

03 / EXPERIENCE OUTLIVES A CONTEXT WINDOW

What should remain
in its attention?

By the middle of the investigation, Tem may have accumulated logs, rejected explanations, useful discoveries and instructions from you. Keeping everything equally prominent would eventually crowd out the next useful thought. Discarding everything old would destroy the thread of the work.

λ-memory makes attention gradual. A memory’s score fades with time since its last access. As the score falls, its representation can become smaller: full text, summary, essence, then a recall reference. Stored material can remain available when the active context no longer contains all of it.

s(t) = Ieff e−λΔtimportance × time-dependent attention

The equation makes an intuitive choice precise: recent, important or recalled material deserves more space. With effective importance held fixed, λ sets how quickly that attention fades.

The mathematics of fading and recall

The implementation uses hours since last access and Ieff = clamp(importance + recall boost, 0.1, 5.0). For λ > 0, the score half-life is ln(2)/λ. At λ = 0.01 per hour, that is 69.3 hours, or 2.89 days. Recall can change the access time and boost; a memory’s whole trajectory need not be one uninterrupted decay curve.

Context pressure raises fidelity thresholds. This is an attention policy, not a measure of factual truth or a guarantee of indefinite archival retention.

Decay and fidelity implementation ↗

Some experience deserves to become a reusable lesson. Tem’s learning-artifact score combines estimated quality, recency and application history. The interesting design choice is that repeated use has diminishing marginal influence.

The mathematics of a reusable lesson
V = αα + β · e−0.015d · [1 + 0.3 ln(1 + u)]

The Beta mean contributes estimated quality, d is days since creation, and u counts applications. Recency has a half-life of about 46.2 days. Model confidence c initializes α = 2 + 3c and β = 2 + 3(1 − c), so for c in [0,1] the initial mean remains between 2/7 and 5/7.

This is a ranking heuristic. Model confidence is not independent evidence, and application counts measure exposure rather than demonstrated benefit. Those distinctions matter when a system learns from its own history.

Artifact score and prior initialization ↗

Memory connects one thought to the next. It also lets a later conversation begin with something of the earlier relationship intact. Yet a memory cannot wake the process that holds it. Tem still needs a relationship with time.

04 / BETWEEN THE CALLS

When should it return?

Suppose the investigation reaches a sensible stopping point: a long operation is running, a scheduled check is due later, or a question needs your input. Continuing to call the model is not automatically progress. The system needs a way to wait and return.

Perpetuum exists because Tem’s intended life extends beyond a foreground conversation. Its temporal machinery supports scheduled work and the ambition of around-the-clock availability. The engineering question is how to make continued presence useful, rather than merely busy.

One small part of that problem is estimating patterns of recorded activity. Tem uses a smoothed frequency for matching local weekday/hour slots:

p̂ = a + 1n + 2active slots and eligible slots, with a Beta(1, 1) prior

If one of four eligible slots contains activity, the estimate is 2/6. The denominator matters: looking only at rows where something happened would make every observed period appear active.

The mathematics—and limits—of the observation window

The current implementation considers matching local weekday/hour slots in the previous 28 completed local calendar days, after the first recorded day. Here a counts active slots and n eligible slots. No eligible slots returns the prior mean, 0.5.

Missing slots currently count as inactive after recording begins. This is not a complete exposure model that distinguishes every offline period from inactivity. Existing UTC buckets also make non-whole-hour timezone offsets approximate. The estimate describes recorded behavior, not verified online presence.

Activity window and denominator ↗

The wider principle is restraint. An enduring entity must be able to act, wait, reconsider and ask. Staying online is infrastructure; knowing what deserves the next moment of intelligence is the continuing design problem.

Now imagine you return in the morning. Tem has remembered, acted and returned to the work. Before trusting its account, you need to ask another question: how does it know the work succeeded?

05 / A VERDICT MUST EARN ITS WEIGHT

“Done” needs a history
you can inspect.

A confident final reply would be easy to produce. The useful result is a reply connected to real actions and the evidence those actions left behind. The model supplies a judgment; the harness must preserve the basis for that judgment.

Witness distinguishes verified, failed and inconclusive checks. Its three-valued logic preserves uncertainty when criteria are combined:

¬? = ?    T ∧ ? = ?    F ∧ ? = F

If evidence is missing, negating the question cannot manufacture success. Under the explicit verification policy, an empty or entirely advisory set of checks remains inconclusive too. This gives “I don’t know” a place in the machinery.

Witness’s composition rules ↗

The same question appears over longer periods: how much evidence should support confidence in a capability? Eigen-Tune’s statistical toolkit makes sample size and stopping rules explicit. Even thirty successes out of thirty trials gives a Wilson lower bound of only about 0.819 at 99% two-sided confidence.

The mathematics of confidence: Wilson intervals
L = p̂ + z²/(2n) − z√[p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/(4n²)]1 + z²/n

Here p̂ = k/n and z is the standard-normal critical value for the chosen two-sided confidence. The checked implementation rejects zero trials, impossible counts and invalid confidence. Suitable binomial observations are still required. An interval cannot make model agreement a ground-truth label or remove correlations between related tasks.

Wilson interval implementation ↗
The mathematics of stopping: sequential evidence
Λn = Σ [xi ln(p₁/p₀) + (1−xi) ln((1−p₁)/(1−p₀))]

The sequential probability ratio test accumulates evidence between two fixed success-rate hypotheses. Its upper boundary is ln((1−β)/α); its lower is ln(β/(1−α)). Reaching the sample cap without crossing either boundary returns Inconclusive.

Here α and β are error targets, distinct from Beta-distribution parameters. Classical error-control arguments depend on assumptions including independent Bernoulli outcomes and fixed hypotheses. They are not blanket guarantees about an adaptive agent.

SPRT and the inconclusive stopping rule ↗

This is why mathematics matters to Tem’s identity. It exposes choices to inspection: the speed of forgetting, the strength of a prior, the threshold for a verdict. It also exposes the limits of those choices. A beautifully computed quantity may still measure the wrong thing.

06 / THE HARDER MEANING OF PERSISTENCE

What if the night
doesn’t go smoothly?

The computer restarts. A request times out after it may already have changed something. A credential expires. Or you choose a different reasoning model halfway through the investigation. These are ordinary events in the life of an agent meant to remain present.

“Never shuts down” is the ambition behind a great deal of reliability engineering. Processes nevertheless stop. The stronger design objective is continuity that survives an interruption: preserve what was intended, what was attempted, what is known to have happened, and what still needs a decision.

Temm1e v6 strengthens scoped conversation storage, saved final replies, resource ownership and cancellation. It avoids blindly repeating uncertain external sends. In the CLI and server, changing the selected model preserves the running state. These are concrete pieces of continuity around a replaceable reasoning engine.

Model and resource ownership ↗

The full ambition goes further. Durable autonomous pursuit still needs more work on task leases, reconciliation and child-task lineage. Current recovery improvements do not guarantee uninterrupted or exactly-once execution.

By morning, the ideal result is more than a completed task. Tem should still know what matters to you, what changed during the work, and where the next conversation begins. Repeated across projects and days, that continuity is what the creator means to build toward with the word entity.

The thought engine may change.
What should remain of Tem?

Calling this architecture AGI-oriented describes its direction: increasingly capable reasoning should have a structure that can carry its actions, experience and commitments forward. It does not claim that Tem has demonstrated AGI, consciousness or personhood. The engineering is already substantial without pretending those questions are settled.

07 / FROM THE IDEA TO THE ACTUAL SYSTEM

Where Tem stands today.

The overnight investigation is a way to understand the design objective, not a report of an end-to-end capability that every configuration already provides. Temm1e’s systems have different maturity levels, and the evidence needs to remain as visible as the ambition.

The v6 modernization paired architectural work with a frozen 30-task coding corpus using the same Z.ai Coding Plan model. Its original scores are:
